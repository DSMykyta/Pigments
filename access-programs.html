Access programs

Access programs

Ordinate medicines reach patients through several access pathways, each governed by national regulation. The right pathway depends on the medicine, the patient's clinical situation and the country in which they live. Some pathways — including restricted institutional access — exist outside the public pharmacy system.

01

Prescription pharmacy

Ordinate prescription medicines reach patients through the country's standard prescription pharmacy system. A licensed clinician issues a prescription based on patient assessment, and the pharmacy dispenses the medicine in accordance with national supply rules. This pathway covers our broadest portfolio: anxiolytics, anti-inflammatories, cardiovascular and metabolic therapies, dermatology, ophthalmology and most pigment integration medicines including Achromavir, Lumenveil, Paxlumen, Oculite-P and Dermavax.

  • Patient assessment by a licensed clinician
  • Dispensed by a registered pharmacy
  • Subject to national medicines supply chain
  • Periodic review by prescriber
02

Hospital supply

Acute-care medicines (Hemovance, Seizuril, Traumexin, Nocirel, Burnaxis) are supplied directly to hospital pharmacies under institutional contract. They are not dispensed in retail pharmacy and are administered or initiated by a hospital-based clinician.

  • Direct supply to hospital pharmacy
  • Administered or initiated in hospital setting
  • Batch-level traceability
  • Hospital pharmacovigilance integration
03

Expanded access

For patients with serious or life-threatening conditions for whom no approved alternative is suitable, Ordinate operates an expanded access program. Requests are made by a treating clinician, reviewed by Ordinate medical affairs and, where applicable, the relevant national regulator. Expanded access is not a substitute for clinical trial participation.

  • Initiated by treating clinician
  • Per-patient or cohort review
  • Subject to national regulation
  • Reporting and follow-up obligations

04

Restricted institutional

Some Ordinate medicines (Saturine, Chromafinil, Pallidex, Calibrant-6, Nullcord, Redline-C) are supplied only through formal institutional programs operated under government supervision. These programs are not part of the public pharmacy system, are governed by bilateral institutional contracts, and are subject to mandatory reporting and oversight.

Process

How a patient or institution makes a request

Identify the appropriate pathway

Use the medicine's product page or the catalog to determine which access pathway applies in your country and audience.

Gather required documentation

For prescription medicines: prescription. For hospital supply: institutional contract reference. For expanded access: clinical justification. For restricted institutional: institution authorization.

Submit through the named channel

Use the medical information form for prescription / hospital / expanded access. Use the institutional access channel for restricted programs.

Receive review and response

Standard responses are issued within five working days. Hospital and institutional supply requests follow contractual timelines.

ImportantAccess pathways and product approvals differ by country. The information above is general; always consult your local Ordinate medical information desk.
