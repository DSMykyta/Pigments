Pediatric ethics

Use of suppression therapies in minors

Children and adolescents require additional safeguards because activation may be triggered by fear, pain, shame, overload or institutional pressure.

Least restrictive care

Stabilization and monitoring are preferred where full blockade is not clinically necessary.

Documented consent

Caregiver permission does not erase the need to hear the adolescent patient.

School support

Medication must not become a substitute for safe school accommodations.

Periodic review

Therapy should be reassessed as control, maturity and environment change.

Registry follow-up

Pediatric exposure and safety outcomes are routed through pharmacovigilance.

Legal escalation

Coercive use requires local legal authority and independent medical review.
