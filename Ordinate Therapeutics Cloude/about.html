About Ordinate Therapeutics

Ordinate Therapeutics is a pharmaceutical company that develops, manufactures and supplies medicines for the regulated coordinates of human physiology. Founded in 2007, headquartered in Amsterdam, with research operations in Cambridge, Yokohama, Lyon and Kyiv.

Mission

Medicine that respects the body it acts on.

Ordinate develops medicines on the principle that the body — pigmented or non-pigmented — is a regulated system whose coordinates must be respected by the medicines that act on it. We refuse to treat pigment carriers as a separate category of patient. We refuse to treat non-carriers as a default. The medicines we make for one population must be safe for the other, and the regulatory frameworks we accept must serve both.

Governance

How Ordinate is governed.

Ordinate is governed by a Supervisory Board, an Executive Board, the Ordinate Safety Committee and an independent Scientific Ethics Council. The Safety Committee has the authority to halt or restrict any Ordinate program — including a profitable one — on grounds of unacceptable misuse potential. The Scientific Ethics Council reviews all pigment medicine programs against autonomy, pediatric protection and institutional-use principles, and publishes its opinion in the annual transparency report.

Supervisory Board

Seven members. Three independent. Two patient-organization observers (non-voting).

Safety Committee

Empowered to halt or restrict programs. Discontinued CRF-12 (Chromafinil civilian derivative) in 2025.

Scientific Ethics Council

Independent advisory body covering pigment autonomy, pediatric protection and restricted-use principles.

Transparency

Annual transparency report

Ordinate publishes an annual transparency report covering: clinical trial outcomes; safety signals and pharmacovigilance actions; aggregate data on restricted institutional programs; pediatric registry summary; pigment-medicine ethical review summary; lobbying disclosures; and supplier diversity.

Position statements

Where we stand.

Pigment autonomy

Suppression, blockade and modulation are medical decisions made by the carrier in consultation with a qualified clinician, within applicable law. Ordinate does not support coercive use of suppression therapies.

Suppression in minors

Pediatric suppression decisions are made within the pediatric supervision framework. Ordinate does not market suppression directly to schools, parents or other non-clinical channels.

Restricted programs

Restricted institutional programs operate under government supervision. Aggregate data is published; individual contracts are confidential. Ordinate accepts independent oversight.

Sport medicine

Recovery and stabilization therapies are supplied under sport-regulator labels. Operational stimulants are not supplied to sport.
