Position statement

Medication and pigment autonomy

Document reference: OTH-POS-AUT-2025 · Approved by the Scientific Ethics Council on 2025-10-14 · Reviewed annually.

Position

Decisions about pigment suppression, blockade or coordinate modulation belong to the carrier, taken in consultation with a qualified clinician and within the boundaries of applicable law.

Ordinate develops, markets and supplies pigment medicine on the principle that medicines acting on a person's pigment expression are not exempt from the standard ethical rules of medical practice — informed consent, proportionality, reversibility where possible, and continuous review.

What this means in practice

  • Ordinate does not market suppression therapies directly to schools, employers, parents or other non-clinical channels.
  • Ordinate's prescribing information specifies that maintenance suppressors and blockers are not intended for coercive use, behavioral compliance or punitive use.
  • Ordinate does not supply public-pharmacy suppressors or blockers to programs whose stated purpose is the social management of carriers outside a medical or legal framework.
  • Ordinate accepts independent oversight of its pigment medicine programs and publishes aggregate transparency data annually.

What we will not do

  • Promote off-label suppression as a "solution" to the existence of pigment carriers.
  • Sell to programs that present pigment expression as a defect.
  • Endorse mandatory suppression of healthy adults outside legally authorized frameworks.

Where the line is drawn

This position acknowledges that there are circumstances in which pigment suppression is medically, procedurally or legally appropriate — surgery, regulated travel, hazardous occupational settings, judicial protocols, emergency response. In each of those, the clinical decision and the legal authorization sit with named, accountable parties. Ordinate's role is to supply medicines that are safe, well-characterized and used as labeled.
