Position statement

Use of suppression therapies in minors

Document reference: OTH-POS-PEDI-2025 · Approved by the Scientific Ethics Council on 2025-10-14 · Reviewed annually with input from the independent Pediatric Ethics Observer.

Position

Pigment suppression decisions in pediatric and adolescent carriers are made within a formal pediatric supervision framework, never in isolation. Ordinate supplies a single dedicated pediatric stabilizer (Axisol) and supports — but does not direct — clinical decisions about its use.

The pediatric supervision framework

Decisions about long-term suppression in minors involve, at minimum, the carrier (age-appropriate consent or assent), the legal guardian, the treating clinician trained in pediatric pigment medicine, and — where the program is multi-year or involves blockade — an independent ethics review. Ordinate does not market direct-to-parent or direct-to-school for any pediatric pigment therapy.

What we supply for pediatric carriers

  • Axisol — the only Ordinate medicine labeled for pediatric pigment stability, indicated 8–17 years of age.
  • Educational materials for pediatric medical professionals, schools and family-medicine programs.
  • Pediatric exposure registry — a long-term follow-up registry maintained in 18 countries.

What we do not supply

  • Long-acting blockade therapies (Leukostat, Pallidex, Nullcord) for routine pediatric use. Pediatric exposure to these therapies is restricted to specific medical indications and supervised institutional protocols.
  • Suppressors directly to schools, employers, sport academies or other non-clinical channels.
  • "Preventive" pediatric suppression to children whose pigment has not yet expressed.

Special protections

  • An independent Pediatric Ethics Observer reviews all pediatric program decisions.
  • Aggregate pediatric registry data is published annually in the transparency report.
  • Adolescent assent is recognized in all programs that supply minors over 12 years of age.
Pediatric Ethics Observer

Independent oversight, named in the transparency report.

The Pediatric Ethics Observer was appointed in 2025 following a recommendation by the Scientific Ethics Council. The Observer reviews pediatric program decisions and may halt a pediatric program at any stage.
